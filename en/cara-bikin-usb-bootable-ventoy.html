Ventoy is a free tool for making a bootable USB that can hold many ISO files at once. One flash drive is enough for Linux, Windows, and other installers — just pick from the boot menu when the computer starts. No need to carry many flash drives, and no need to burn again every time you switch ISO.

Figure 1: The Ventoy bootable USB workflow — install, copy ISOs, boot.


What Is Ventoy



Ventoy (ventoy.net) is a free tool for making a multi-ISO bootable USB. With Ventoy, one flash drive can hold many ISO files — Linux, Windows, and more — and you just pick which ISO to boot from the menu that appears when the computer starts. The ISO files are simply copied onto the flash drive like regular files, so adding or removing an ISO is as easy as copying and deleting a file.

What You Need



- A flash drive — its contents will be erased when you install Ventoy, so use an empty flash drive or back up its contents first.
- The ISO file of the operating system you want to boot, for example a Linux or Windows ISO.
- A computer to run the Ventoy installer.
- Download Ventoy at ventoy.net.

Step 1: Install Ventoy on the Flash Drive



Download Ventoy from ventoy.net, then run the installer. Select your flash drive in the device list, then click Install. Wait until the process finishes.
Warning: Installing Ventoy erases ALL contents of the flash drive. Back up your important files before installing. Make sure the device you pick is really your flash drive — picking the wrong device can erase another disk.

Step 2: Fill It with ISOs



Once Ventoy is installed, the flash drive is ready to fill. Copy ISO files onto the flash drive like regular files — just drag and drop. No need to burn again: to add a new ISO just copy it, to remove an ISO you no longer need just delete the file.
Tip: Give the ISO files clear names so they're easy to pick in the boot menu later.

Step 3: Boot from USB



Plug the flash drive into the computer you want to boot, then turn it on or restart it. Set the boot order in BIOS/UEFI so USB comes first, or open the boot menu and select your flash drive. After that the Ventoy menu appears — pick the ISO you want to boot, and the computer will run from that ISO.
Tip: If the Ventoy menu doesn't appear, check the boot order in BIOS/UEFI again — make sure the flash drive is first in line.

Flash Drive Not Detected on Your Phone



Ventoy has no official app on Android, so there is no official way to create or manage a Ventoy USB directly from a phone. A common problem: flash drives formatted exFAT+MBR are often not detected on phones. The FAT32 format is more compatible on phones, but ISO files over 4 GB can't be stored on FAT32.

Figure 2: FAT32 vs exFAT — pick what fits your needs.


So the honest choice is this: use FAT32 if you want the flash drive detected on your phone and your ISO is under 4 GB. Use exFAT if your ISO is large, but be ready for the flash drive not to be detected on your phone.
Tip: Use FAT32 if your ISO is under 4 GB and you want the flash drive detected on your phone. Use exFAT if your ISO is large — but be ready for the flash drive not to be detected on your phone.

Tips



- Back up the flash drive's contents before installing Ventoy — installation erases everything.
- Keep the ISO files you use often on the flash drive; ones you no longer need can just be deleted like regular files.
- Check the flash drive's format (FAT32 or exFAT) if the flash drive isn't read on your phone — see the section above.
- Download Ventoy from its official site at ventoy.net.
