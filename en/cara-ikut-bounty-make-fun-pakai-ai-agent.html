make.fun is a bounty platform: someone puts up a prize in USDC for a program — usually a web app, a game, or a small tool — and participants build the program to match the brief. Submitted programs are judged by a house judge, and the top-ranked winners share the prize. The interesting part is that you don't have to code it yourself: an AI agent can build the program for you — your job is to direct it, test it, and pick the best one before submitting.

What Is make.fun



Picture a competition notice board: every bounty lists a title, a task description (the brief), the prize amount, the deadline, and how many programs have already been submitted. You pick one bounty, build the program, and submit it before the deadline. Once the deadline passes, the house judge scores all submitted programs and picks the top-N winners — top 10 or top 50, for example, depending on the bounty.
Prizes are paid in USDC, a cryptocurrency pegged to the US dollar. That's why you need a crypto wallet to receive a prize — covered in the next section.
Tip: New bounties appear on make.fun every day, so get in the habit of checking the bounty list regularly. New bounties with big prizes usually fill up fast.

Sign Up and Set Up a Wallet



First step: create an account on make.fun. Then connect a Solana wallet — this is the wallet used to receive your prize if you win. Create a new wallet if you don't have one yet, and write down its seed phrase somewhere safe (on paper, not as a screenshot on your phone).
Your wallet address looks like <your-wallet> — a long string of letters and numbers. It's fine to share this address (for example, to receive a prize), but your seed phrase and private key must never be shared with anyone, under any circumstances.
Warning: Your seed phrase and private key are the keys to your wallet. Anyone who has them can drain it. Never type them into any website, form, or chat — including to someone claiming to be from the make.fun team.

How to Read a Bounty



Before joining, read the bounty page all the way through. Here's what you must pay attention to:
- Title and brief: what needs to be built. A clear brief usually names the type of program (a game, a website, or a tool, for example) and the judging criteria.
- Prize: the total prize in USDC and how many winners share it (top 10, top 50, and so on). A bigger prize isn't necessarily easier — it usually attracts more competition too.
- Deadline: the submission cutoff. Don't start building if there isn't enough time — an unfinished program can't be judged.
- Number of submissions: how many programs have already been submitted. This number gives you a sense of how crowded the competition is.
- Entry fee: some bounties are free and some are paid. Check before joining — if there's an entry fee, make sure you're actually willing to pay it. Free bounties are the safe place to start.

Step-by-Step: How to Join a Bounty


Figure 1: The flow of joining a bounty on make.fun — from signing up to having your program judged.


Step 1: Sign up on make.fun and connect your Solana wallet. Create an account, then connect the wallet you prepared. Make sure the wallet is active and that you can open it again any time.
Step 2: Read the bounty list. Look at the title, prize, deadline, and number of submissions. Pick a bounty whose prize is worth the time you have — and whose brief you understand.
Step 3: Read the brief all the way through and study past winners. If a similar bounty has already finished, look at the winning programs: what made them win? Was the design clean, the features complete, or the idea unique? Copy the pattern, not the code.
Step 4: Build the program with an AI agent. Explain the brief to your AI agent clearly — what needs to be built, which features are required, and how it should look. Ask the agent to build the program, then test it yourself in the browser: click every button, try every feature, and fix what's broken. Don't trust the first result blindly.
Step 5: Submit before the deadline and save your claim code. Once the program is finished and tested, submit it through the bounty page before the deadline passes. Save the claim code you receive after submitting — it's proof of your participation and is used to check the results later.

Tips to Get the Judge's Attention


Figure 2: Build 3 samples with different ideas, compare them, then submit the best one.


Build 3 different samples, then pick the best. Don't get stuck on your first idea. Ask the AI agent to build three versions with different approaches — one focused on looks, one on features, one on a wild idea, for example. Compare all three, then submit the strongest.
Test your program yourself before submitting. Open it as if you were the judge: is its purpose obvious at a glance? Is there a button that doesn't work? Does it look good on both phone and laptop screens? A program you've tested yourself is almost always more polished than one sent straight away.
Watch the entry fee and the deadline. Prioritize free bounties when you're just starting out. Note the deadline of every bounty you join, and leave time for testing — don't submit at the last minute.
Watch the daily submission limit. There's a limit on how many programs you can submit per day. Spend your daily slots on the most promising bounties, not on every bounty out there.
Tip: Winning programs are usually not the most complex ones, but the ones with the clearest purpose and the easiest to use. Simple but finished and polished often beats complex but half-done.

What to Avoid



- Don't submit a program you haven't tested. A buggy or half-finished program is obvious when judged, and will almost certainly lose to one that has been tested.
- Don't use real money for trading or coin bounties. Some bounties involve trading or launching a coin with real money — the risk is real and the money can be lost. Stick to bounties about building programs, especially when you're just starting out.
- Don't share your private key or seed phrase with anyone. There is no legitimate reason to share them — not with an admin, not with a support team, not with anyone.
- Don't chase every bounty at once. One polished program is better than five sloppy ones.
Warning: If a bounty asks you to spend a large amount of money, connect your wallet to a site you don't know, or share your wallet keys — stop and leave that bounty. The prize isn't worth the risk of losing everything in your wallet.
